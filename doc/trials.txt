<h2>Introduction
    This is a specification for a trial of the feasibility of using
    random variant vector graphic shape designs for Snakes
    for the game snakes and ladders.

<h2>Display Layout
    500 x 600px stage with white background, for drawing snakes (details below).
    Next button for re-drawing the snakes.

<h2>Snake Designs
    Eight random variant vector graphic snake shape designs should be displayed.

    The snakes should be from 90 to 432 pixels in length, including the forked
    tongue at the head. They should be sinusoidal within a range of 34px and a 
    maximum of 25px wide.

    The snakes should lie vertically with a maximum offset from the vertical
    at head or tail of 108px.

    The snakes should not overlap.

    The snakes should be bright colours, decorated with horizontal bands, spots,
    rings, diamond shapes or lengthwise squiggles.

<h2>Display Layout

    The stage should be 360 x 600px, with a yellowish off-white background and a grid 
    of 8 x 11 square(45 x 45px) grey bordered cells aligned with the top of the stage.

    Nine snakes using the methods for variants established at 
    Snake-Stage-5-Variants should appear on the board randomly such that:
    1) The tip of the tongue and the end of the tail sit in the centre of
        a grid square.
    2) The snake is aligned vertically, with a random variation such that
        the tail falls into a column one cell to the left or one cell
        to the right of the head.
    3) The snake is not less than three cells high.
    4) The snake is not more than nine cells high.
    5) The snake does not extend beyond the edges of the board (grid)
    6) No two snakes have heads or tails in the same cell
    7) Snakes do not touch or cross each other
    8) Snakes may appear in the same column, but not in contact with
        each other.

    There should be a Next Layout button below the stage.

<h2>Display Layout
    Six ladders are to be added at random to the board. These are reddish-brown
    and 32px wide. They are layed out as follows:

    1) The ladders begin and end in the centre of a grid square,

    2) The minimum vertical height of a ladder is 90px (3 grid squares, centre
     to centre).

    3) The maximum vertical height of a ladder is 360px (9 grid squares, centre
     to centre).

    4) Like the snakes, the ladders can be offset at the base by -1, 0 or +1 cell

    5) Ladders should not begin or end on the same grid cell as the start or end
        of other ladders or snakes.

    6) Ladders should not end in the top left cell.

    7) Ladders should not start in the bottom left cell.

    8) Ladders should not cross snakes or other ladders.

<h2>Display Layout
    The display area should be 360px wide centred for PC's and mobiles.

<h3>Introductory Overlay
    The introductory overlay appears when the app is first loaded, it
    gives a description of the game. It has a Game Start button to clear
    the overlay and begin the game.

<h3>Menu Bar 
    The menu bar appears at the top of the display, it has a logo on the
    left and the button options Restart and Home on the right.

    The Restart presents a new board and game.

    The Home button sets the browser on ../index.html

<h3>Stage
    The board and dice area is prepared and presented as established.

    We now add the player's and the computer's pieces to the bottom
    left hand cell (1) of the board.

    For the pieces, use konva vector graphics as per the example
    (tidied-up) in the attached samplePiece.zip file (like a chess pawn).

    The pieces should be 25px high, red with black outline for the player
    white with black outline for the computer. The pieces should overlay
    the snakes and ladders and the numbers.

    The pieces should stick to lanes in the grid rows, the computer on 
    the top third of a cell, the player's in the bottom (overlaying the 
    computer's when on the same cell).

<h3>Status Line 
    This appears below the stage, with text centred. It tells the player
    whose turn it is and prompts the player to touch/click the die.

<h3>Win/Lose Overlay
    The Win/Lose overlay panel is displayed when the computer/player's 
    piece reaches cell 88.

    It displays the appropriate message.

    The overlay has a Restart Game button, to start the game from scratch.
    
<h2>Game Play 

    The starting player is selected at random, and then take alternate
    turns.

    When it is the computer's turn, the status line displays "Computer's 
    turn", the die spins and the piece is moved the matching number of
    cells as a hopping animation, following the numbering of the board.

    If the piece ends on a snake's top, it is shown as an animation
    sliding down the snake to the end.

    If the piece ends on a ladder end, it is shown as an animation sliding 
    up the ladder to its top.

    If the player/computer rolls a six, they get an additional turn 
    (regardless of whether they have slid down a snake or up a ladder).

    On the top row, a player must get an exact roll to get to the top right
    cell (88), if their throw exceeds this, they remain on the same cell.

    When the player gets to land on cell 88 he is the winner, and the win 
    overlay is displayed. Otherwise, if the computer gets to cell 88, the
    lose overlay is displayed.
